### §1.2 Three recurring patterns

**Pattern P1 (bridge-direction monochromaticity).** In every instance
in every class, after normalization, *every* T2$\to$T1 bridge is blue.
Concretely, among the 56 witnesses the T2$\to$T1 bridge color counts
$(R, B)$ averaged:

|------|---|---:|
| C1 | $(0.00, 5.00)$ | 8/8 |
| C2a | $(0.00, 3.00)$ | 8/8 |
| C2b | $(0.00, 2.00)$ | 8/8 |
| C2c | $(0.00, 3.00)$ | 8/8 |
| C3a | $(0.00, 3.00)$ | 8/8 |
| C3b | $(0.00, 3.00)$ | 8/8 |
| C3c | $(0.00, 3.00)$ | 8/8 |

The T1$\to$T2 bridges are *not* uniformly monochromatic (averages around
$(2, 2)$); they "absorb" the color deficit. The asymmetry is real and
not a labelling artifact: the deficit-aware generator places b21
bridges to satisfy a smaller demand (each non-interface vertex of $T_2$
needs 1 in-arc to reach $d^- = 3$, summing typically to $|S_2^n|$
or one less), so b21 is the *narrow* side of the bridge multiset and
the SAT solver routes one full strong-spanning-subdigraph color through
it.

**Pattern P2 (tight 3-cut compartment dichotomy).** Tight 3-cuts
$\delta^+(X)$ in a gluing fall into a small number of "compartment
signatures" by which $V$-partition each of the three cut-arcs crosses.

tight 3-cuts:

Two signatures are *deterministic* given the color labelling normalization:

- $\{\,(\,S_2^n,\,S_2^n)\,,\,(\,S_2^n,\,S_2^n)\,,\,(\,S_2^n,\,S_1^n)\,\}$:
  every such tight cut has two side-2-internal arcs of one color and
  one b21-bridge of the other (15/15).
- $\{\,(\,S_2^n,\,I)\,,\,(\,S_2^n,\,I)\,,\,(\,S_2^n,\,S_1^n)\,\}$:
  two arcs from $S_2^n$ into $I$ of one color and one b21-bridge of the
  other (15/15).

Both signatures are *witnesses to the same vertex-cut*: a singleton
$X = \{v\}$ with $v \in S_2^n$ and $d^+(v) = 3$, whose three out-arcs
either go entirely inside $T_2$ ($S_2^n$-internal arcs and an arc into
$I$) or hit one b21 bridge into $S_1^n$. In all such cases the b21
bridge sits in the minority color (after normalization, $R$ has 2
versus $B$ has 1, since I normalized b21 to be $B$; this means the
singleton out-cut splits $(2, 1)$ as it must, with the b21 bridge alone
in $B$).

**Pattern P3 (degree-3 vertex out-cuts are (2,1) for free).** For every
vertex $v$ with $d^+(v) = 3$ in any witness, the three out-arcs split
exactly $(2, 1)$. Across the 15 instances this is 100/100 on side-1
non-interface, 50/50 on side-2 non-interface (degree-3 case). This is
just the SAD-equivalent restatement of the definition — every directed
cut, including each singleton out-cut, receives at least one arc of
each color — but it sharpens to **the (2,1) split is forced exactly
when $d^+(v) = 3$.** No witness violates this on a degree-3 vertex.

### §1.3 Interpretation

The three patterns together are a *local* statement: at each tight
3-cut, the color split is essentially determined by a 1-bit choice
("which color is the majority on this cut?") and that bit is
*compatible* across overlapping cuts in our gluings. P1 and P2
furthermore say that the choice on $\delta^+(X)$ when $X$ separates
$S_2^n$ from $S_1^n \cup I$ is **made by the bridges**: a single
direction (b21) carries one color, so any cut $X$ with the bridge in
$\delta^+(X)$ is forced. This is exactly the role of the *splitting
arcs* in Bang-Jensen–Wang Lemma 2.5: each splitting arc is a *single*
color, and its lift to the original pair $(s_1 v, v s_2)$ pushes that
single color through the shell vertex $v$.

CL1's hypothesis should therefore not be (the round-1 version) "color
compatibility at lifted vertices" but rather a stronger *cut-coloring
constraint at the gluing interface*: the bridges between the two parts
of $V$ must be assignable to two color classes such that for **every**
tight 3-cut whose three arcs split across the two parts, the color
split forced by the bridge assignment is consistent with the local
strong-decomposition structure on each part.

## §2. CL1 statement

I state CL1 in the **decomposable-parts** shape (a $V$-bipartition with
decomposable inner parts, glued by bridges), not the "splitting-off"
shape of round 1. The decomposable-parts shape is the one the Phase 3
v2 witnesses live in, and it specializes cleanly to Bang-Jensen–Wang
Lemma 2.5 when the parts are $D[V_1]$ and $D[V_2]$ of a split digraph.

**Notation.** For $X \subseteq V$ write $\partial X = \delta^+(X) \cup
\delta^-(X)$ for the *boundary* arc set (arcs with exactly one endpoint
in $X$); $\delta^+(X)$ for out-arcs, $\delta^-(X)$ for in-arcs.
"Strong arc decomposition" (SAD) of $D$ means an arc-partition
$A(D) = A_1 \,\dot\cup\, A_2$ such that both $(V, A_1)$ and
$(V, A_2)$ are strongly connected.

**Lemma CL1 (controlled lifting at a 3-arc interface).** Let
$D = (V, A)$ be a 3-arc-strong digraph. Suppose there is a non-trivial
vertex bipartition $V = V_1 \,\dot\cup\, V_2$ with $|V_i| \geq 2$,
$\partial V_1 = \partial V_2 = B^+ \cup B^-$ where
$B^+ = \delta^+(V_1) = \delta^-(V_2)$ is the set of **bridges**
$V_1 \to V_2$ and $B^- = \delta^+(V_2) = \delta^-(V_1)$ is the set of
bridges $V_2 \to V_1$. Write $D_i = D[V_i]$ for $i = 1, 2$. Suppose:

1. *(decomposable inner parts).* Each $D_i$ admits a strong arc
   decomposition $A(D_i) = R_i \,\dot\cup\, B_i$ (red and blue arc
   classes; $i$ ranges over $\{1, 2\}$, so $R_1, B_1$ live in $V_1$
   and $R_2, B_2$ in $V_2$).
2. *(bridge minimum).* $|B^+| \geq 2$ and $|B^-| \geq 2$.
3. *(direction monochromatic bridges).* There is a partition
   $B^+ = B^+_R \,\dot\cup\, B^+_B$ and $B^- = B^-_R \,\dot\cup\, B^-_B$
   into red and blue bridges such that:
   - **at least one bridge in each direction lies in each color**:
     $B^+_R, B^+_B, B^-_R, B^-_B$ are all non-empty;
   - **(local coverage at each non-interface vertex).** For every
     $v \in V_i$ with $d_D^+(v) = 3$ or $d_D^-(v) = 3$, the three
     out-arcs (resp. in-arcs) of $v$ split exactly $(2, 1)$ in the
     combined coloring $R_i \cup R_{i'}$ and $B_i \cup B_{i'}$ where
     $i' \neq i$ is the opposite part (i.e. the bridge incident at $v$,
     if any, lies in the minority color of $v$'s out- or in-cut).
4. *(no monochromatic tight 3-cut at the interface).* No tight
   3-cut $\delta^+(X)$, $\emptyset \neq X \subsetneq V$, with
   $X \cap V_1 \neq \emptyset$ and $X \cap V_2 \neq \emptyset$, is
   monochromatic in the combined coloring.

Then $D$ admits a strong arc decomposition with color classes
$A_R = R_1 \cup R_2 \cup B^+_R \cup B^-_R$ and $A_B = B_1 \cup B_2 \cup
B^+_B \cup B^-_B$.

**Remarks on the hypotheses.**

- $k = 3$ is the working value; the lemma is stated for 3-arc-strong $D$
  because (a) hypothesis (3) uses $d_D^\pm(v) = 3$ as the local-coverage
  trigger, and (b) hypothesis (2) is automatic from 3-arc-strongness if
  $V_1, V_2$ are both non-trivial and the bipartition cuts $\delta^+(V_1),
  \delta^-(V_1)$ are tight at value 3; the asymmetric case where one
  bridge direction has 4 arcs is also covered. The lemma probably holds
  at $k = 2$ under the same hypotheses, but at $k = 2$ the *existence*
  of the bipartition with two decomposable parts is essentially
  equivalent to the full conjecture (any 2-arc-strong $S_4$-free
  example), so calling out $k = 3$ is honest.

- Hypothesis (3) is the **direction monochromatic bridges** condition:
  both directions of bridge get colored, and each contains both colors,
  but the color of each *individual* bridge is fixed by a global pairing
  with the local strong-decomposition deficits at endpoints. This is
  the empirical pattern P1+P3, raised to a hypothesis.

- Hypothesis (4) is the **technically heavy** one; §3 reduces it to a
  manageable verification (the only cuts to check are those with
  $|X \cap V_i| < |V_i|$ on both sides, and they form a laminar-like
  family controlled by the strong-decomposition trees inside each $D_i$).
  This is where the v3-attack-plan candidate left a TODO.

- The round-1 splitting-off formulation of CL1 in
  `02_structural_program.md` §1 is recovered when each
  $V_i = V_2 \cup \{v\}$ for a single shell vertex $v \in V_1 = S$
  and the "bridges" reduce to a single pair $(s_1 v, v s_2)$ per
  split-off arc; hypothesis (3)'s "minority bridge inherits the
  minority color" becomes "both arcs of the lifted pair inherit the
  color of the splitting arc," i.e. Bang-Jensen–Wang's verbatim
  Lemma 2.5 rule.

## §3. Proof sketch

Let $D, V_1, V_2, R_i, B_i, B^\pm_R, B^\pm_B$ be as in CL1.

**Step 3.a (color classes are spanning).** $A_R = R_1 \cup R_2 \cup
B^+_R \cup B^-_R$ contains, by hypothesis (1), every vertex of $V_1$
as an endpoint of an $R_1$-arc and every vertex of $V_2$ as an endpoint
of an $R_2$-arc, hence spans $V$. Same for $A_B$.

**Step 3.b (each color class is strong).** Take $A_R$ (the argument for
$A_B$ is symmetric). I show every directed cut $\delta_{A_R}^+(X)$ for
$\emptyset \neq X \subsetneq V$ is non-empty.

Split $X$ as $X_1 = X \cap V_1$, $X_2 = X \cap V_2$. Consider three
cases.

*Case A: $X_2 = \emptyset$ (so $X = X_1 \subsetneq V_1$).* Then
$\delta_D^+(X) \subseteq A(D_1) \cup B^+$. In particular
$\delta_{A_R}^+(X) \supseteq \delta_{R_1}^+(X_1)$. By hypothesis (1)
applied to $D_1$ and its strong-decomposition class $R_1$, the
out-cut $\delta_{R_1}^+(X_1)$ is non-empty unless $X_1 = V_1$ or
$X_1 = \emptyset$. If $X_1 = V_1$, $X = V_1$, and
$\delta_{A_R}^+(V_1) = B^+_R$ which is non-empty by (3).

*Case B: $X_1 = \emptyset$ (so $X = X_2 \subsetneq V_2$).* Symmetric
to Case A using $R_2$ and $B^-_R$.

*Case C: $X_1, X_2$ both non-empty.* Now $\delta_D^+(X)$ contains arcs
of four kinds:
- arcs $u \to v$ inside $D_1$ with $u \in X_1, v \in V_1 \setminus X_1$;
- arcs $u \to v$ inside $D_2$ with $u \in X_2, v \in V_2 \setminus X_2$;
- bridges $u \to v$ in $B^+$ with $u \in X_1, v \in V_2 \setminus X_2$;
- bridges $u \to v$ in $B^-$ with $u \in X_2, v \in V_1 \setminus X_1$.

The first two sets are the out-cuts $\delta_{D_1}^+(X_1)$ and
$\delta_{D_2}^+(X_2)$; the third is $\delta^+(X_1) \cap B^+ \setminus
\delta^+(X_2)$; the fourth is $\delta^+(X_2) \cap B^- \setminus
\delta^+(X_1)$.

By hypothesis (1) plus the SAD-coloring of $D_1$:
$\delta_{D_1}^+(X_1)$ has at least one $R_1$-arc unless $X_1$ is
$\emptyset$ or $V_1$. Same for $\delta_{D_2}^+(X_2)$. Provided that
**neither** "unless" clause triggers, $\delta_{A_R}^+(X)$ is non-empty.

The two boundary subcases (i) $X_1 = V_1$ (with $X_2 \neq \emptyset,
V_2$), (ii) $X_2 = \emptyset$ (covered by Case B) or symmetric, all
reduce to checking the bridge cut. For (i),
$\delta_D^+(X) = \delta_{D_2}^+(X_2) \cup (B^- \setminus
\delta^-(V_1 \setminus X_1)) \dots$ — wait, I need to be more careful.

If $X_1 = V_1$ and $X_2 \subsetneq V_2$, then arcs out of $X$ are
exactly arcs $u \to v$ with $u \in V \setminus (V_2 \setminus X_2) =
V_1 \cup X_2$ and $v \in V_2 \setminus X_2$. These are
$\delta_{D_2}^+(X_2)$ and the bridges $B^+ \to V_2 \setminus X_2$,
which is *all of* $B^+$ minus those landing in $X_2$. The full
$\delta_D^+(X)$ contains at least one $R$-arc because $\delta_{D_2}
^+(X_2)$ is non-empty in $R_2$ by hypothesis (1) on $D_2$.

The genuinely hard subcase is when $X_1$ and $X_2$ are both *small*
relative to their parts (specifically, when the boundary $\partial X$
within each part is small enough that hypothesis (1) does not yield an
$R$-arc inside $X_i$'s slice). This is exactly the **propagation
step** mentioned in `02_structural_program.md` T2.

**Step 3.c (propagation: hypothesis (4) closes the loop).** Hypothesis
(4) directly assumes that no tight 3-cut $\delta^+(X)$ with $X$ meeting
both parts is monochromatic. Hence in Case C above, if both
$\delta_{D_i}^+(X_i)$ contributions are mono-blue (i.e. lie entirely
in $B_i$, $i = 1, 2$), and the two bridge contributions are also
blue, then $\delta^+(X)$ is mono-blue *and* tight at exactly the
quantity $|\delta_{D_1}^+(X_1)| + |\delta_{D_2}^+(X_2)| + |\text{bridge
contributions}|$. If this quantity is exactly 3, hypothesis (4) is
violated; if greater, the cut is not tight and we still need to
exclude monochromaticity.

**This is where the proof has a gap.** Hypothesis (4) covers tight
3-cuts only. A non-tight cut with size $\geq 4$ in $D$ could still be
monochromatic in $A_R$ if the SAD-decompositions on $V_1, V_2$ happen
to push all $R$-arcs in $\delta_D^+(X_1)$ and $\delta_D^+(X_2)$ into
the *intersection* of the two cut definitions, leaving the bridge
boundary $B$-colored. The witnesses make this seem unlikely (no such
cut was observed in 56 witnesses) but the proof as written does not
exclude it.

**Gap statement.** The remaining step in the proof of CL1 is to show
that hypotheses (1)–(3) plus *the strong-decomposition structure on
each $D_i$* together rule out monochromatic cuts $\delta^+(X)$ with
$X \cap V_1 \neq \emptyset, V_1$ and $X \cap V_2 \neq \emptyset, V_2$
and $|\delta^+(X)| \geq 4$. Equivalently: **if every tight 3-cut at the
interface is hypothesis-(4)-controlled, are all higher-size cuts
automatically bichromatic?**

The empirical answer in 56 v2 witnesses is "yes, no monochromatic cut
of any size was observed," but I have not turned this into a proof.
Three candidate proof routes:

- (R1) *Submodular reduction.* If $\delta_{A_R}^+(X)$ is monochromatic
  blue for some $X$ with $X_1, X_2$ both proper-non-empty, then by
  submodularity of $|\delta^+(\cdot)|$ one of $\delta^+(X_1)$ or
  $\delta^+(X \cup V_1)$ is also tight; iterate until reaching a tight
  3-cut where hypothesis (4) applies.
- (R2) *Branch-witness argument.* Each $R_i$ contains a spanning
  out-branching $T^+_i$ and a spanning in-branching $T^-_i$ rooted at
  some $r_i \in V_i$. Glue them via $B^+_R$ and $B^-_R$ to obtain a
  spanning out-branching of $D$ in $A_R$; conclude every vertex has an
  $R$-path from $r_1$ (say) and hence every cut $\delta^+(X)$ with
  $r_1 \notin X$ contains an $R$-arc. The dual gives the rest.
- (R3) *2-SAT-style closure.* Phrase the cut-coloring as 2-SAT over
  laminar-tight cuts; hypothesis (4) is exactly the local
  satisfiability constraint, and the laminar structure forces global
  consistency.

Route (R2) is the most concrete and is the line of attack in
Bang-Jensen–Wang Lemma 2.4. I think it works for CL1; the careful
write-up needs one more pass.

## §4. Tests of CL1 on the v2 corpus

For each of the seven pair-classes I check whether CL1's hypotheses
are satisfied on the SAT witnesses.

**Hypothesis (1) — decomposable inner parts.** Both $V_1 = V(T_1)$ and
$V_2 = V(T_2)$ are 2-arc-strong UNSAT templates by construction
(`05_audit.md` confirms each). So $D[V_1]$ and $D[V_2]$ do *not*
admit a SAD as standalone 2-arc-strong digraphs.

**This is a problem for the CL1 statement as written.** The lemma
hypothesis (1) requires each part to admit a SAD; the gluings of two
UNSAT templates by definition do not satisfy it on the templates
themselves. The Phase-3-v2 SAT verdict on the *glued* $D$ is consistent
with the lemma *only* if the gluing modifies each $D_i$ in a way that
makes it admit a SAD — e.g., the bridges contribute to in/out
connectivity inside each $D_i$ via the interface.

This is the precise sense in which my round-1 CL1 was too narrow: I
defined the inner parts as $D[V_i]$ for the *original* template, but
the deficit-aware gluing actually upgrades each template's interface
vertices' connectivity. The *correct* inner parts for the lemma to
apply to a Phase-3-v2 gluing are $D[V_i \cup I]$ for $i = 1, 2$ — each
includes the interface as a shared boundary. Bridges then become
properly bipartite (between $S_1^n$ and $S_2^n$, not between $V_1$ and
$V_2$).

**CL1 (revised partition).** Re-state hypothesis (1) with
$V_1 = S_1^n$, $V_2 = S_2^n$, and the *interface* $I$ as a third
piece whose arcs $D[I]$ are shared (counted toward both parts'
strong-decompositions, but allocated to color classes by a unique
choice). Hypothesis (4) then applies to cuts $X$ with $X \cap S_1^n
\neq \emptyset$ *and* $X \cap S_2^n \neq \emptyset$; the interface
plays the role of a "shared kernel" rather than an inner part.

Under this revision:

- **Hypothesis (1) on $S_1^n \cup I$.** This is $D[V(T_1)]$, the
  original 2-arc-strong template. *Still UNSAT.* So the SAD-on-each-
  part hypothesis is not literally available to derive the Phase-3-v2
  SAT verdicts.
- **What is true** is that each $D[S_i^n \cup I]$ *admits a partial
  coloring* (one out-branching from a chosen root) using $T_i$'s arcs
  plus the interface arcs, which together with the bridges produce a
  full SAD on $D$.

So CL1 as written above is *not the right vehicle* for the deficit-
aware gluings; it is the right vehicle for cases where each part is
itself SAD-decomposable (e.g., when each $T_i$ is 3-arc-strong on its
own, which the v2 gluings deliberately violate).

**Re-positioning.** CL1 in its present form is a publishable lemma
**for the situation where the parts are SAD-decomposable on their
own** — a setting that arises naturally in lifting from one solved
class to a new class (e.g., gluing a semicomplete kernel to a
locally-semicomplete shell). The v2 corpus does *not* directly test
CL1 in this regime, because the test instances were chosen as
gluings of 2-arc-strong **UNSAT** templates precisely to stress the
search. The honest takeaway from §1 is:

> The Phase 3 v2 witnesses **do not falsify CL1** but they do not
> verify CL1 either, because they violate hypothesis (1) on the chosen
> partition. They *do* support the *form* of hypotheses (3) and (4):
> empirically, bridges decompose by direction into one majority color
> each, and tight 3-cuts at the interface are uniformly bichromatic.

**A consequence for Phase 4 planning.** The next round should
**generate test instances by gluing pairs of *3-arc-strong*
templates**, not 2-arc-strong UNSAT templates, in order to verify
CL1 directly. The simplest test bed is: take two 3-arc-strong
semicomplete digraphs (each SAD by BJ–Yeo 2004 plus the standard
$\geq 3$-arc-strong upgrade), glue them along a 3-vertex interface
with the deficit-aware bridge multiset, and check that the resulting
gluing's SAT witness satisfies hypotheses (3) and (4). Predict: yes.

**Coder action item (carried to §5).** Add a Vehicle-6 generator
that glues 3-arc-strong members of the four solved classes
(semicomplete, locally semicomplete, semicomplete compositions, split)
along 3-arc interfaces, run the verifier, and pull the SAT witnesses.
This is the *positive* test bench for CL1.
