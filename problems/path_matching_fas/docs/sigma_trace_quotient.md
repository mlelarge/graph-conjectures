# D69: Low-Hit Sigma-Trace Quotient

## 1. Target

The D68 synthesis identifies the bag ordering \(\sigma\) as the
remaining bottleneck in the J-pathwidth DP.  The full state is

\[
  (\sigma,\deg,\Pi),
\]

where \(\Pi\) is the loaded-backedge component partition on the bag.
The partition term is large, but the factorial \(|B|!\) sigma term is
larger and dominates once the bag has size at least 6.

This note tests and partially proves the next compression:

> keep only the low-hit insertion traces of \(\sigma\), not the whole
> permutation.

The implementation is
[`scripts/sigma_trace_quotient_probe.py`](../scripts/sigma_trace_quotient_probe.py).
Tests are in
[`tests/test_sigma_trace_quotient.py`](../tests/test_sigma_trace_quotient.py).

## 2. Definition

Let \(S=(\sigma,\deg,\Pi)\) be a full J-DP state at a bag \(B\), and
let \(x\) be a vertex not yet introduced by the fixed nice path
decomposition.

For a cut \(c\) of \(\sigma\), let
\[
  H_x(c)=\{u\in B:\{x,u\}\in E(J)\text{ and the }T\text{-arc on }
  \{x,u\}\text{ is backward after inserting }x\text{ at }c\}.
\]

The cut \(c\) is **low-hit feasible** if:

1. \(c\) respects every forced score-window inequality between \(x\)
   and the current bag;
2. \(|H_x(c)|\le 2\);
3. every \(u\in H_x(c)\) has \(\deg(u)\le 1\);
4. if \(H_x(c)=\{u,v\}\), then \(u\) and \(v\) are in different
   \(\Pi\)-components.

The **low-hit trace** records, for every future vertex \(x\), the set
of feasible hit sets \(H_x(c)\).  The strongest implemented variants
also retain hit order or cut adjacency, but the main tested quotient is
the coarsest:

\[
  Q_{\rm set}(S)=
  \bigl(B,\deg|_B,\Pi|_B,\{(x,\{H_x(c):c\text{ feasible}\})\}_x\bigr).
\]

It forgets all cuts with \(>2\) hits, and it forgets the order of bag
vertices except where that order changes the feasible hit sets.

## 3. Proved Local Lemma

**Lemma 3.1 (Immediate-transition soundness).**  Fix a full J-DP state
\(S=(\sigma,\deg,\Pi)\), a future vertex \(x\), and a cut \(c\) of
\(\sigma\).  Inserting \(x\) at \(c\) is accepted by the J-DP introduce
transition if and only if \(c\) is low-hit feasible.

**Proof.**  The J-DP introduce transition checks exactly three
conditions after the forced-order admissibility of the cut:

* the new vertex \(x\) receives one loaded edge for each backward
  J-neighbour in the current bag, so \(\deg(x)=|H_x(c)|\); hence
  \(|H_x(c)|\le 2\) is necessary and sufficient for \(x\)'s degree;
* each hit endpoint \(u\in H_x(c)\) has its degree increased by one,
  so the endpoint degree test is exactly \(\deg(u)\le 1\);
* a new loaded edge closes a cycle exactly when its endpoints already
  lie in the same loaded-backedge component.  With one hit, this cannot
  occur because \(x\) is new.  With two hits \(\{u,v\}\), the two loads
  create a cycle exactly when \(u\) and \(v\) are already in the same
  \(\Pi\)-component.

These are precisely the clauses in the definition of low-hit
feasibility. ∎

**Corollary 3.2.**  For a fixed state \(S\), the low-hit trace
determines the set of immediately surviving introduce moves, up to
forgetting which cut generated a duplicated hit set.

This is the part of the sigma-compression proof that closes cleanly.

## 4. What Remains Unproved

The missing global lemma is not immediate-transition soundness.  It is
trace evolution:

> **Open Lemma 4.1 (Trace evolution).**  If two full states have the
> same low-hit trace quotient and one introduces \(x\) through a
> feasible hit event \(H\), then there is a corresponding introduce
> move in the other state whose child has the same low-hit trace
> quotient.

Without Lemma 4.1, the quotient is not a proved DP state.  The reason
is that a hit set may be generated by several cuts, and different cuts
can leave different relative orders between \(x\) and the old bag
vertices.  Those relative orders can affect the traces of later future
vertices.

So the status is:

* immediate transition: proved;
* extension-equivalence / bisimulation: open.

## 5. Empirical Result

The exact probe enumerates the full J-DP state graph, computes winning
states by a backward pass, then groups full states by the low-hit trace
quotient.  A mixed winning/losing quotient class is a real refutation.

No mixed quotient class was found in the following tests:

| family | checked | max full states | max quotient classes | collision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `one_block` n=12 | 1 | 180 | 178 | no |
| `skew_induction` n=12 | 1 | 110 | 98 | no |
| `wake1_failure` n=12 | 1 | 967 | 816 | no |
| n=7 minimal NO catalogue | 20 | 72 | 72 | no |
| random n=7 | 200 | — | — | no |
| random n=8 | 50 | — | — | no |
| random n=9 | 30 | — | — | no |
| random n=10 | 10 | — | — | no |

The quotient survives the known `one_block` collision that refuted the
dormant-matching aggregate, and it survives the minimal-NO catalogue.

## 6. Negative Runtime Signal

The compression is weak.  On `one_block`, 180 full states collapse to
178 quotient classes.  On `wake1_failure`, 967 collapse to 816.  On
the n=7 minimal-NO catalogue, the maximum is 72 full states and 72
quotient classes.

This means the low-hit trace is probably close to reconstructing
\(\sigma\) on hard bags.  It may still be structurally useful, but it
does not yet give a plausible polynomial state bound.

## 7. Verdict

The next move is partially proved:

* **Proved:** the low-hit trace is exactly the information needed for
  one introduce transition's degree and cycle tests.
* **Empirically supported:** no extension-equivalence collision on the
  known hard substrates.
* **Not proved:** trace evolution / bisimulation.
* **Runtime concern:** the quotient barely compresses full \(\sigma\)
  on the tested hard instances.

The next mathematical target is therefore sharper:

> Either prove Trace Evolution Lemma 4.1, or prove that the low-hit
> trace reconstructs \(\sigma\) on a broad class of hard bags, making
> this quotient too fine for a polynomial algorithm.

If Lemma 4.1 fails, the first failure is the right flex-edge-only
hardness substrate.
